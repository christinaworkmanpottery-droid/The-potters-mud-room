# Why Your Pottery Cracks While Drying: 7 Beginner Mistakes and How to Prevent Them

**Level:** Beginner  
**SEO keywords:** why pottery cracks while drying, prevent clay cracking, beginner pottery mistakes, drying pottery evenly

You finish a beautiful bowl, mug, or handbuilt dish, then return to find a crack. It is one of the most frustrating beginner pottery problems, but it is often preventable. Clay shrinks as water leaves it, and uneven shrinkage puts stress on weak areas. Here are seven common mistakes to check before your next project.

## 1. Drying your pottery too quickly

Placing fresh pottery in direct sun, near a heater, or in a strong draft can dry the surface before the interior. Slow the process by loosely covering the piece with plastic and gradually increasing airflow.

## 2. Leaving uneven wall thickness

A thick base attached to thin walls dries at a different rate. When throwing or handbuilding, aim for consistent thickness and pay particular attention to the transition between the floor and walls.

## 3. Forgetting to compress slabs and bases

Compression helps align clay particles and reduce weak spots. Use a rib to compress slab surfaces and the floors of wheel-thrown forms, especially wide plates and shallow dishes.

## 4. Joining pieces when moisture levels differ

A wet handle attached to a nearly leather-hard mug may shrink differently and pull away. Let the parts reach compatible firmness, score and slip appropriately for your clay body, and support the join while it sets.

## 5. Letting handles and rims dry first

Thin attachments and exposed rims lose moisture quickly. Wrap delicate areas selectively, or tent the entire piece, so the body and attachments dry more evenly.

## 6. Moving clay before it is strong enough

Fresh pieces can bend or develop stress cracks when lifted. Use a bat or board for support and wait until the clay is firm enough to handle without distortion.

## 7. Ignoring the clay body's behavior

Different clay bodies have different shrinkage rates and working characteristics. Record which clay you used, how thick the piece was, and how you dried it. Patterns become easier to spot over time.

## A simple beginner drying routine

Start with a level, absorbent surface. Cover new work loosely, check for moisture differences daily, and uncover it in stages. Avoid rushing the final drying stage. A piece must be thoroughly bone dry before bisque firing; residual moisture can cause dangerous steam-related breakage in the kiln.
